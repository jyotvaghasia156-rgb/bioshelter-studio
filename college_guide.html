Bioclimatic Architectural Physics & Closed-Loop Life-Support Engineering

A rigorous 4-credit collegiate course curriculum designed for undergraduate and graduate civil engineers, architectural physicists, and disaster relief planners.

1. Course Syllabus & Educational Objectives

By completing this curriculum using BioShelter Studio, students master zero-energy passive heating and cooling, transient thermal mass heat equations, Kusuda harmonic subsoil geothermics, and ASHRAE 55 / ISO 7730 human thermal comfort modeling.

Unit I: Solar Geometry & Microclimates

Celestial sun-path azimuth, solar altitude angle (α), direct normal irradiance (DNI), and psychrometric enthalpy analysis.

Unit II: Geothermal & Soil Physics

Kusuda subsoil harmonic temperature attenuation, soil thermal conductivity (k), volumetric heat capacity, and Ground-to-Air Heat Transfer (GAHT).

Unit III: Transient Envelope Physics

Multi-layer composite U-values, periodic thermal lag (φ), decrement factor (f), and thermal mass water buffering.

Unit IV: Closed-Loop Regenerative Bio-Loops

Nitrosomonas / Nitrobacter biological nitrification cycle, symbiotic fish waste-to-vegetable harvest ratios, and CO2 photosynthesis dynamics.

2. Mathematical Foundations & Thermal Lag Calculus

In passive solar bioshelters, thermal inertia delays heat transmission through dense earthen envelope assemblies. For a homogeneous masonry monolith of thickness L, thermal diffusivity α = k / (ρ • cp), subjected to periodic diurnal solar radiation of period τ = 24 hours:

Thermal Diffusivity: α = k / (ρ • c_p) [m²/s]
Periodic Thermal Lag: φ = (0.5 • L • √(86400 / (π • α))) / 3600 [hours]
Decrement Factor: f = exp(-0.25 • φ) [dimensionless ratio]

Example Verification: A 300mm Rammed Earth Monolith (k = 1.25 W/m•K, ρ = 2100 kg/m³, cp = 1050 J/kg•K) yields α = 5.67 × 10-7 m²/s, producing an exact analytical time lag φ = 9.18 hours and decrement factor f = 0.10. Peak solar heat penetrating at 13:00 reaches the interior at 22:11, exactly when night heating is required.

3. Subsurface Geothermal Kusuda Equation

Earth temperature at depth z (meters) on day t (0 to 365) is computed using the semi-infinite solid heat conduction formulation with periodic surface harmonic boundary condition:

T(z, t) = T_mean - A_s • exp(-z √(π / (α • 365))) • cos( (2π/365) • (t - t_0 - (z/2) √(365 / (π • α))) )

Where T_mean is annual mean surface ambient temperature (°C), A_s is annual surface temperature amplitude (°C), α is soil thermal diffusivity (m²/day), and t_0 is day of minimum surface temperature.

6. Hands-on Engineering Laboratory Practicals (6 Labs)

Lab 1: Measuring Thermal Lag & Decrement of Earthen Envelopes

Duration: 3 Hours

Objective: Compare 300mm Rammed Earth, 200mm CEB Earthbag, and 16mm Polycarbonate under 1,000 W/m² halogen solar simulator in BioShelter Studio. Calculate time lag Δt and amplitude damping.

Lab 2: GAHT Earth Tube Sizing & Soil Geothermal Purge

Duration: 3 Hours

Objective: Design a 120m corrugated HDPE subsurface air tube network at 2.5m depth. Measure convective heat transfer coefficient ($h$) and air exit temperature drop under 45°C ambient inlet air.

Lab 3: Closed-Loop Aquaponic Nitrogen Equilibrium

Duration: 2 Hours

Objective: Calibrate the stoichiometric biological conversion of un-ionized ammonia ($NH_3$) to nitrite ($NO_2^-$) and nitrate ($NO_3^-$) for a 6.5m³ fish tank supporting 85m² of vegetable crop beds.

Geothermal Integration Accurate Kusuda harmonic calculation with ≥ 8.0h envelope thermal lag. Soil depth sized correctly; partial damping accounted for. Unshaded envelope with zero thermal mass buffer.
